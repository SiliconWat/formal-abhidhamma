### 2. Derivations
**D1 — the sun and moon at the same distance.** If A16 + P1, then d☉/d☾ = 1. Measured: the Moon by lunar laser ranging (384,400 km); the Sun by Venus radar ranging and direct solar radar (Stanford 1959): 1.496×10⁸ km. d☉/d☾ ≈ 389. **COLLIDES.** A yojana ≈ 9.15 km fits the Moon alone; no length fits both, because the text fixes the ratio at 1.

**D2 — apparent size.** If A16 + A17 + P1, θ ≈ d/h: θ☾ = 49/42000 rad ≈ 4.0′, θ☉ = 50/42000 rad ≈ 4.1′. Measured: Moon 29.3′–34.1′, Sun 31.6′–32.7′; known since antiquity (Aristarchus: ~½°). The texts' discs appear ≈ 7.8× too small. **COLLIDES, unit-free.**

**D3 — solar eclipses.** If A16 + A17, θ☾/θ☉ = 49/50 for every observer seeing both at the same distance — and at EQUAL HEIGHTS the two can never lie on one line of sight: two points at the same height on one ray from the ground are the same point. So the Moon can never cover the Sun: no total eclipse. Measured: total and annular eclipses both occur (e.g. the totality of 8 April 2024), θ☾/θ☉ ranging 0.92–1.08, Saros predictions from the lunar orbit accurate to seconds. **COLLIDES.** The texts give eclipses another cause — Rāhu (SN 2.9–2.10, RECALLED, not verified).

**D4 — the Sun's brightness.** Received flux = surface brightness × the disc's solid angle. To deliver the measured 1361 W/m² from Ω = π(25/42000)² sr, the surface brightness must be (32.0′/4.09′)² ≈ 61× the Sun's; for a blackbody, T ≈ 5772 K × 61^¼ ≈ 16,100 K.
