**2. Derivations**

**D1 — apparent size. COLLIDES.** θ_moon = 49/42,000 ≈ 1.17×10⁻³ rad ≈ 4.0′; θ_sun ≈ 4.1′ — a ratio, whatever the yojana. Measured: Moon 29.3–34.1′, Sun 31.5–32.7′. The texts predict discs ≈ 7.7× too small. The only escape is magnifying the sky, which P1 forbids; a uniform ×7.7 magnification would also stretch the sun's 180° horizon-to-horizon path beyond what a hemisphere holds; a lens around the discs only is a new, untested addition.

**D2 — the sun's distance against the moon's. COLLIDES.** By A16, r_sun/r_moon ≈ 42,075/42,000 ≈ 1.002; measured ≈ 389 (lunar laser ranging, ≈ 2.56 s round trip; Venus radar + spacecraft tracking, the IAU 2012 AU). Fixing the yojana at ≈ 9.15 km to match the Moon puts the Sun at ≈ 3.85×10⁵ km — SOHO, at the sunward L1 point ≈ 1.5×10⁶ km away, would lie BEYOND the Sun looking back at it. No single yojana satisfies both.

**D3 — diurnal order: stars fastest, then sun, then moon (:8353). AGREES.** Measured: sidereal day 86,164 s, solar day 86,400 s, mean lunar day ≈ 89,400 s. Physics explains the order by Earth's rotation plus orbital motions; the texts state it as motion of the discs. The order is right; the mechanism is not tested here.

**D4 — Rāhu occults the sun from inside its path. COLLIDES.** An occulter touching the disc has no parallax against it, so every observer who sees the sun would see the same eclipse, at the same moment, of the same size (Δφ = 0 for all observers). Measured: totality falls in a narrow band (≈ 115 km wide on 2017-08-21) that sweeps across Earth, predicted by Besselian elements. Second collision: the covering edge in a partial eclipse, and Baily's beads at contact, match the Moon's limb profile from laser altimetry (Kaguya, LRO) — not a hand, jaw or tongue.

**D5 — Rāhu occults the moon. AGREES in one feature** — a lunar eclipse looks the same at the same moment to everyone on the night side, as a touching occulter predicts. COLLIDES on two others: lunar eclipses only at full moon, solar only at new moon near a node, recurring on the saros; and the shadow's edge is a circular arc of Earth's shadow radius. A Rāhu driven by envy (*issāpakata*) has no timetable; for the texts to hold he must act exactly on the node geometry — for timing alone that makes Rāhu indistinguishable from geometry (philosophy); D4 still separates them.

**D6 — Rāhu cannot slow the discs. AGREES** — eclipses do not change the Sun's or Moon's motion.

**D7 — Rāhu's body during totality. COLLIDES, conditionally** (opaque and near the sun): at 42,000 yojanas a 4,800-yojana body subtends ≈ 6.5°; during totality stars within ≈ 1–2° of the Sun were photographed and measured (Eddington 1919). In daylight a dark body above the atmosphere is invisible, so the collision applies only during totality.

**D8 — the stars (:8349). COLLIDES, conditionally** (stars at comparable height, "on both sides" — implied, not stated numerically): a 7–12-yojana disc would subtend ≈ 0.6–1′; measured stellar diameters are milliarcseconds (Michelson interferometry). Holding the texts' sizes needs h ≳ 5×10⁷ yojanas.

**3. Distinguishing measurements** — all already made: angular diameters; laser ranging + the AU; eclipse-path geometry and limb profiles; the 1919 plates; stellar interferometry. **No change of physics inside A0 clears D1, D2 or D4: each collides with a measurement already made.** D1 and D2 hold whatever the yojana. For the texts to hold, physics would need all at once: light not travelling straight near the discs (vs D1); ranging that does not measure distance (vs D2); an occulter with lunar topography on the ecliptic (vs D4). The one exit is outside A0 — the discs as known through their characteristics (the experiential reading) — philosophy, not physics.

**4. Contradictions among the axioms and texts**
- The two commentarial layers disagree on optics: the older commentary (:8297) says the colour reaches the eye, the Atthasālinī (:8301) says it does not. The older view agrees with physics; the author's ruling is a claim about cognition — philosophy.
- The stacking needs an unstated assumption: A16's 100-yojana stack (49 + 1 + 50) works only if each disc is as thick as it is wide; A17 gives only length and width — implicit, not a contradiction.
- The heights are not fixed: the ṭīkā says 42,000 holds for the first kappa only — D1 and D2 slightly time-varying; clears neither.
