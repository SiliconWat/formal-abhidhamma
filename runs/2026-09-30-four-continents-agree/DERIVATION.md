## 2. Derivations

### D0 — the texts' own configuration (guard 2e)
- Four continents sit around Meru at 90°: Pubbavideha east, Aparagoyāna west, Uttarakuru north, Jambudīpa south.
- Reading the rows of `:833`, the noon order is U → P → J → A. That is N → E → S → W, clockwise seen from above, which matches *padakkhiṇa* (`s0103t:833`).
- The sun's height is 42,000 yojanas (A16, R6), its radius R_s changes with the season (`:829`, R4), and one circuit takes a day.
- The ground is flat: R4's distances are plane distances.
- Nothing in the texts I read names what makes night for a continent. R2 only states the rule |Δ| ≤ 90°.

### D1 — the table is the time-zone relation
- **Plain English.** The four rows are one rule rotated four times. Sunrise at X is noon at the continent one quarter-turn ahead, sunset at the one two quarters ahead, and midnight at the one three quarters ahead. All 16 entries are consistent.
- **Maths.** Let k: {J, P, U, A} → ℤ/4 with J↦0, P↦1, U↦2, A↦3. Local phase at X is φ(X, t) = φ(J, t) + k(X)·(τ/4), where τ is one day.
- **Verdict: AGREES.** On a rotating sphere, local solar time is UT + λ/15° per hour. Four stations 90° of longitude apart show exactly this table. The table is also exactly what four stations on the equator see at the equinox (D2). The texts' clockwise order also agrees with the sun's apparent clockwise motion about the north celestial pole seen from above.
- **Measurement:** none needed, because it agrees.

### D2 — the three/two rule fixes day length at half a circuit, in every season
- **Plain English.** Under R2, three continents see the sun only at the exact quarter instants and two at all other times, just as four equatorial stations do at the equinox. That part agrees. But the rule makes sunrise-to-sunset always half a circuit.
  - With even revolution (the texts' own *samaṃ parivattanti*, `vin01t1:4145`), every day and every night at every continent lasts 12 h, all year.
  - Without assuming evenness, write q_XY for the time the sun takes from over X to over Y. Then:
    - day(J) = q_PJ + q_JA
    - day(J) + day(U) = τ
    - day(P) + day(A) = τ
- **Maths.** vis(X, t) ⟺ |Δ_X(t)| ≤ π/2. If φ(t) = ωt, then day(X) = π/ω = τ/2 for every X and every season.
- **Verdict: COLLIDES at the solstices; AGREES at the equinox.**
  - At the equinox the texts give 12 h 00 m. Measured equinox day length is about 12 h 06–08 m.
  - Measured day length at a fixed place follows cos H₀ = −tan φ·tan δ. At Sri Lanka (~7°N) it runs from about 11 h 43 m to 12 h 31 m; at Bodh Gaya (24.7°N), from about 10 h 35 m to 13 h 40 m. Sunrise and sunset almanacs have met these values for centuries.
  - The corpus also holds the other option (guard 2g): R7 says the cold season has long nights.
- **Ad hoc fix that clears D2 alone:** an uneven circuit timed to the seasons. It leaves three problems.
  1. The texts tie unevenness to the righteousness of kings (`vin01t1:4141`), not to the calendar.
  2. It requires day(U) = τ − day(J).
  3. It predicts day length would depend on politics and drought. Measured sunrise and sunset times repeat every year within minutes, whatever the monsoon.
- **Distinguishing measurement:** day length at one Jambudīpa site at both solstices. It has already been made.

### D3 — on a flat plane, the texts' sunrise is always north of east
- **Plain English.** Put Meru at the origin and J at (0, −R_o). The sun is at (R_s·sin Δ, −R_s·cos Δ, h). At the texts' sunrise Δ = 90°, so the direction from J to the sun has a northward component R_o − R_s·cos Δ = R_o > 0. This holds for any radii and any height, in any season.
- **Maths.** At sunrise the azimuth is north of east by α = arctan(R_o/R_s) > 0.

  | Sun's path | Texts' sunrise (Sri Lanka) | Measured at 7°N |
  |---|---|---|
  | Middle path (equinox) | 40.5° north of east | due east |
  | Near-rim path (December) | 30.8° north of east | about 23.6° south of east |
  | Near-Meru path (June) | 56.5° north of east | about 23.6° north of east |

- **Also:** the observer is inside the sun's circle (R_o < R_s at the middle path). The flat model therefore has the sun's azimuth sweep about 261° during the equinox day. Measured: 180° (east through south to west), and a gnomon's shadow tip traces a straight line at the equinox.
- **Verdict: COLLIDES.** This contradicts every sunrise-azimuth observation from about 23 September to 20 March, and the straight equinox shadow line. It is a collision with appearances, so it survives any change of window (A0 → A0′).
- **Distinguishing measurement:** sunrise azimuth at any Jambudīpa site in December. It has already been made.

### D4 — on a flat plane, apparent size ∝ sin(elevation)
- **Plain English.** If the sun stays at height h over flat ground, its distance is D = h / sin ε, where ε is its elevation. Its apparent size is then θ ≈ (s/h)·sin ε. R1's half-disc on the horizon means ε → 0 at rising, so the disc would shrink toward nothing.
  - With the texts' numbers at the equinox: D_noon ≈ 62,766 and D_rise ≈ 425,563 yojanas. The texts' "sunrise" happens at ε = 5.66°, not 0°, where the disc would be 0.148 of its noon size.
  - With A17's s = 50 yojanas (ADDED): θ_noon ≈ 2.7′ and θ_rise ≈ 24″.
- **Maths.** θ(ε) / θ(π/2) = sin ε. The half-disc condition (ε = 0) and a constant size (θ_rise / θ_noon ≈ 1) cannot both hold for any h, s or radii. This result does not depend on A16.
- **Verdict: COLLIDES.** The sun's horizontal diameter is measured at about 31.6′–32.7′. Its variation within a day is below about 4×10⁻⁵ (Earth's radius over one astronomical unit). The flattening near the horizon affects the vertical diameter only.
  - Separately, A16 + A17 give an absolute size of 2.7′, against 32′ measured.
- **Ad hoc fix:** a distance-cancelling magnification M(D) ∝ D, applied to the sun and moon, clears the size problem. It leaves:
  - the half-disc cut (magnification makes no horizon);
  - D3;
  - the moon's measured 1.7% augmentation (larger at the zenith than at the horizon), which M ∝ D erases.
- **Distinguishing measurement:** already made (solar diameter at the horizon vs at noon).

### D5 — noon altitude with the texts' own numbers
- **Maths.** ε_noon = arctan(h / |R_s − R_o|).

  | Season | Texts (Sri Lanka) | Measured at 7°N |
  |---|---|---|
  | Equinox | 42.0° | 83° |
  | June | 24.2°, to the north | 73.6°, to the north |
  | December | 12.7° | 59.6° |

- **Partial agreement:** the texts place the sun overhead at Sri Lanka about two months either side of Āsāḷha (`vin01t1:4293`). Measured zenith passages at 7°N are about 12 April and 31 August, roughly June 21 ± 70 days. The radial swing is the texts' version of solar declination and agrees at the month level.
- **Verdict: COLLIDES** on noon altitude. Gnomon shadow lengths have been measured since antiquity. Raising h would fix noon, but it lifts the "sunrise" elevation (sin ε_rise = h / √(h² + R_o² + R_s²)) and so worsens D4. D4 and D5 close jointly for flat ground with straight rays.

### D6 — the texts' own occluder, tested (guard 2b)
Night needs an occluder. At the equinox R3's 900,000-yojana reach covers the whole far continent (about 597,000 yojanas from the sun), so a finite reach cannot be what makes night at the equinox.
- **(a) Meru alone** (84,000 wide, 84,000 high, taller than the sun's 42,000). The line of sight passes Meru's centre at distance g(Δ) = R_o·R_s·sin Δ / √(R_o² + R_s² − 2R_o·R_s·cos Δ).
  - With g < 42,000 (cylinder) or < 59,397 (square half-diagonal), the dark arc is about 33°–47°, which is **2.2–3.1 h of night**, not 12 h.
  - **COLLIDES with the texts' own R2**, which requires 12 h.
- **(b) Yugandhara as a ring of radius R_Y, with its top at the sun's height** (R5, R6). For R2's 90° rule at the equinox from Sri Lanka, R_Y = g(90°) = R_o·R_s / √(R_o² + R_s²) ≈ 209,180.
  - In Āsāḷha the sun runs at R_s = 181,931 < R_Y, at the ring's own height. Every line of sight from outside would then be blocked, and Sri Lanka would see no sun all June.
  - Seeing the June sun requires R_Y < 181,931. The equinox 90° rule requires R_Y ≈ 209,180. **With the texts' own radii, the two requirements contradict each other.**
  - Possible clearing: the texts' *nāgavīthi* ascent (`s0103t:829`, `vin01t1:4137`) could lift the June sun above the ring, but no magnitude is given. The texts tie that ascent to rain and to kings, not to the calendar. It would therefore predict the June sun vanishing in drought years (*ajavīthi*, sun lowered). Ephemerides and almanacs show no such effect.
- **(c) Seasonal sign.** ∂g/∂R_s at Δ = 90° equals R_o³ / (R_o² + R_s²)^{3/2} > 0. Any fixed ring therefore gives longer days when the sun is on the outer path, which the texts place in the cold months (`:829`).
  - At Sri Lanka g(90°) runs from about 151,760 (June) to about 236,420 (December).
  - **COLLIDES in sign:** northern-hemisphere days are shortest in December. R7, the corpus's own grammar, agrees with the measurement.
  - This result does not depend on R_Y.
- **(d) Meru as an opaque occluder** is 84,000 high and 233,219 yojanas north of Sri Lanka's centre. It fills the northern sky up to 19.8° elevation.
  - **COLLIDES:** Polaris is observed from Sri Lanka at about 7° elevation (navigators' latitude rule).
  - This survives any change of window, because it is about what is seen.
- **Closure (guard 2g):** no fixed occluder inside A0 clears (a), (b), (c) and (d) together. A season-tracking ring would clear (b) and (c) but is not in the texts and leaves (d).

### D7 — "at one stroke" and the speed of light
- **Maths.** Δt = (D_rise − D_noon) / c ≈ 362,797 yojanas × 13 km / c ≈ **15.7 s** (13.7–19.4 s across the yojana range in P3).
- **Reading 1:** *ekavelāya*, "at one time" (R1's own gloss). At human time-grain there is no collision. Verdict: AGREES, conditional. The small point that remains: flat geometry makes the sunrise continent see the sun where it was about 16 s earlier, about 3.9′ behind in its circuit. A sphere keeps the difference between stations at 21 ms or less, and cross-station solar ephemerides agree to about 1″.
- **Reading 2:** Vism's *ekakkhaṇe*, read strictly as one mind-moment. This reading is OURS, using A24. If a finger-snap lasts 0.1–0.3 s (premise), a mind-moment is at most about 3×10⁻¹³ s. The light would then need v ≳ 10²² m/s ≈ 5×10¹³ c. Verdict: COLLIDES, with Rømer's light-time and radar ranging.
- The choice between readings locates the collision; it does not rescue one.

### D8 — what physics would have to be
- Keeping D1–D2's table, R1's half-disc, and the measured appearances (due-east equinox sunrise, constant apparent size, Polaris above the northern horizon) requires that, optically, observers sit on a sphere.
- **Named construction:** transformation optics, in which an optical medium conformally equivalent to a sphere makes the half-space above the plane look like the outside of a ball (the Möbius and Maxwell fish-eye family). It clears D3, D4, D5 and D6(d) together, and also the timing of light signals.
- **What it leaves:** mechanical measurements. The Foucault pendulum precesses at 15°·sin φ per hour, plumb lines converge (deflection-of-the-vertical geodesy), and there is Coriolis deflection.
- If the medium is extended to all of mechanics and gravity too, then no measurement separates the flat plane from the sphere. The plane becomes a coordinate chart of the sphere, and the texts' yojana figures (233,218¾ from Meru to Sri Lanka; the Sun's height of 42,000) stop being distances a ruler could measure. **At that point: none — philosophy.**
- Joint statement: no addition inside A0 that keeps flat ground, straight rays and the texts' 90° rule clears D3 and D4 together.

## 3. Where the axioms and readings contradict each other
1. **A22 + R2 (12 h all year) against R7** (the cold season has long nights). This is corpus against corpus, across genres.
2. **A22 + R2 against `:829`** (the seasonal path, same commentary, four lines up). Combined with any fixed occluder, the seasonal radius makes day length vary. R2 forbids that. The two paragraphs agree only if the edge of darkness follows the season, and no text names such a mechanism.
3. **R2 + R5/R6 + R4.** The texts' own occluder (Yugandhara at the sun's height) cannot give 12 h at the equinox and a visible June sun at the same time (D6b).
4. **A16 + R1 + P1.** A sun at 42,000 yojanas never reaches 0° elevation inside the cakkavāḷa: the minimum is about 2.3°, seen from the far rim. So the half-disc requires an occluder. Meru is too small for 12 h of night (D6a).
5. **R3 (900,000-yojana reach) against R3's own occlusion reason.** The reach does not cause night at the equinox, and the author's own reason for darkness is occlusion. The two are consistent with each other, but the reach is idle.

## 5. Theses for the refuter
1. D1: A22's table is one ℤ/4 rotation and is exactly the time-zone relation for four stations 90° of longitude apart (AGREES).
2. D2: A22 + R2 fix day(J) + day(U) = τ, and with even revolution 12 h everywhere all year. This collides with measured solstice day lengths and with the corpus's own *dīgharatti = hemanta*.
3. D3: on any flat Meru plane, the texts' sunrise is strictly north of east (40.5° at the equinox from Sri Lanka). Measured: due east at the equinox and south of east all winter.
4. D4: flat ground gives θ ∝ sin ε, so the ṭīkā's half-disc on the horizon and the measured constant solar diameter cannot hold together.
5. D5: with the texts' own radii and h = 42,000, the equinox noon sun at Sri Lanka stands at 42°. Measured: 83°.
6. D6a: Meru alone gives 2.2–3.1 h of night, not the 12 h that R2 requires.

9. D6d: an opaque Meru would hide the northern sky below 19.8° from Sri Lanka. Polaris is seen there at about 7°.

11. D8: only an optics conformally equivalent to a sphere clears D3–D6 together inside A0. Extended to mechanics, it turns the texts' yojana figures into chart coordinates — philosophy.
