## 1. Derivations

**D1. The texts' own eclipse ratio (derived first, per guard 2e). COLLIDES.**
- Plain English:
  - The moon's centre is at H. The sun's centre is 24.5 + 1 + 25 = 50.5 yojanas higher (B1 + A16).
  - For any observer whose line of sight passes through both centres, similar triangles make the distance ratio the same: (H + 50.5)/H, wherever the observer stands.
  - So the moon can never cover the sun. Totality would need H ≤ 2,474.5 yojanas. The ṭīkā's "a few yojanas less" does not come close.
  - Whether the discs are flat or solid does not change the ratio: B1 makes both 3-D, and any shape factor applies to both.
- Maths:
  - θ☾/θ☉ = (49/50)·(H + 50.5)/H = 0.98 × 1.0012 = 0.9812 at H = 42,000
  - θ☾/θ☉ ≥ 1 ⇔ H ≤ 49 × 50.5 = 2,474.5
- **COLLIDES** with observed total solar eclipses: 1919 Sobral/Príncipe, 2017-08-21, 2024-04-08, totality lasting up to about 7.5 min. The measured θ☾/θ☉ ranges from about 0.90 to 1.08.
- What physics would have to add: an optical element confined to the one-yojana gap that shrinks the sun's image by at least 2% relative to the moon's. On its own this clears D1. It does NOT clear D2, D3 or D5 below.
- Distinguishing measurement: totality itself, already made.

**D2. The distance ratio. COLLIDES, and it is unit-free.**
- Plain English: the texts put the sun only 0.12% farther away than the moon. Parallax and ranging put it about 389 times farther.
- Maths: d☉/d☾ (texts) = 1.0012; measured = π☾/π☉ = 3422″/8.794″ ≈ 389.
- **COLLIDES** with:
  - lunar laser ranging (mm-level);
  - planetary radar and spacecraft ranging, which fix the AU at 149,597,870.7 km;
  - the classical horizontal parallaxes (moon ≈ 57′, sun ≈ 8.79″).
- A gap lens (D1's fix) does not touch this. No change of physics inside A0 clears it: the collision is with measurements already made.

**D3. Angular size. COLLIDES, and it is independent of the yojana's length.**
- Plain English:
  - 49 yojanas seen from 42,000 yojanas is about 4′, and 50 from 42,050.5 is about 4.1′. The measured sizes are about 31′ and 32′: roughly 7.8 times larger.
  - Fitting the measured sizes would need heights of about 5,400 yojanas, or discs of about 380 yojanas.
- Maths:
  - θ☾ = 49/42,000 = 1.167×10⁻³ rad = 4.01′ (measured 29.3′–34.1′)
  - θ☉ = 50/42,050.5 = 1.189×10⁻³ rad = 4.09′ (measured 31.6′–32.7′)
  - ratio ≈ 7.8; H_fit ≈ 49/0.00905 ≈ 5,400
- **COLLIDES** with heliometer and modern solar-radius measurements (959.6″ from SDO and from eclipse contact timings) and with the lunar semidiameter in ephemerides. The magnification escape is closed in D6.

**D4. Phases from the texts' OWN mechanism (B3, B4 + P2). COLLIDES. Also an internal contradiction (§3).**
- Plain English:
  - The ṭīkā says sunlight falls on the moon from above, and the sun is always above the moon (A16). So an observer on the ground below never sees the moon's visible face fully lit.
  - For an observer directly below the moon, the lit fraction is (1 − sin ε)/2, where ε is the sun's elevation seen from the moon. That is 0 at new moon, which AGREES with the new moon being unseen. But it rises to about ½ as soon as the sun is a few hundred yojanas away sideways: under 5 minutes at the texts' 100,000 yojanas a day. After that it stays just below ½, and never reaches full.
  - At the same instant, the phase depends strongly on where the observer stands. Observers on the side away from the sun see a crescent; only observers near the horizon on the sun's side see it nearly full.
- Maths:
  - α = 90° + ε, so k = (1 − sin ε)/2 < ½ for all ε ∈ (0, 90°]
  - ε ≤ 10° once L ≥ 50.5/tan 10° ≈ 286 yojanas, reached at t ≈ 286/10⁵ day ≈ 4 min
  - an off-zenith observer at angle z: k = (1 ∓ sin(z ± ε))/2, which varies across [0, ~1] at one instant
  - phase angle 0 needs a ground point H·L/50.5 beyond the moon, about 10⁸–10⁹ yojanas at full moon, outside the cakkavāḷa
- **COLLIDES** with:
  - the full moon being fully lit at culmination (phase angle a few degrees at most; opposition-surge photometry);
  - phase being the same for every observer on Earth (topocentric difference at most about 1°, from lunar parallax);
  - illuminated fraction following (1 − cos elongation)/2 smoothly over 14.77 days.
- The texts' alternative (guard 2b): the "moon's own shadow" (*chāyā*) as an intrinsic pattern, i.e. a self-luminous moon. Added as an axiom and tested, it COLLIDES with:
  - the lunar spectrum being reflected sunlight, with the solar Fraunhofer lines;
  - crater-shadow lengths tracking the local sun elevation (LRO imaging);
  - Diviner thermal maps: about 390 K on the sunlit side and about 100 K on the dark side, following the sun;
  - earthshine brightness tracking Earth's cloud albedo (Big Bear Earthshine project).

**D5. A monthly eclipse every new moon (A16 + A17 + B3 + B6 + P1). COLLIDES.**
- Plain English:
  - At every new moon the texts put the opaque silver moon directly under the sun (B3's bowl).
  - An observer directly beneath loses about 96% of the sun's disc (an annular eclipse). Partial overlap reaches everyone who sees the pair within about 78.5° of the zenith.
  - The texts' "lamp in daylight" (glare) is about visibility. It does not make an opaque body transparent.
- Maths:
  - covered area fraction = (0.9812)² ≈ 0.963
  - overlap condition: 50.5·sin z / s < 49.5 / s ⇔ sin z < 0.980 ⇔ z < 78.5°
- **COLLIDES** with the eclipse record. Solar eclipses happen only when new moon falls near a lunar node: about 2–5 a year against about 12.4 new moons, with Saros recurrence every 6,585.32 days. Most new moons eclipse nowhere on Earth.
- The only exit is a transparent moon. That contradicts B6 ("silver-covered"). It also removes the moon as an occulter altogether, so every solar eclipse would have to be Rāhu's, which D7 tests.

**D6. Disc-crossing time: magnification cannot rescue D3 (P3 + B5 + A14). COLLIDES.**
- Plain English:
  - The time the sun's disc takes to move its own width, t = D/v⊥, is unchanged by any smooth, static optics: a lens scales the disc and its motion alike.
  - In the texts the sun circles Sineru once a day (B5), at a radius at least half of Meru's width (A14). The ṭīkā at s0103t.tik.xml:833 puts the "near Sineru" path well outside Meru's edge.
  - For an observer the sun passes overhead, that gives about 16 s. The measured meridian transit of the sun's disc at equinox is about 128 s.
- Maths:
  - t = D/v, with v = 2πr/T and r ≥ 42,000
  - t ≤ 50 × 86,400 / (2π × 42,000) = 16.4 s (17.1 s with the texts' π = 3)
  - measured: 32′/(15′ per minute) = 2.13 min ≈ 128 s at δ = 0
- **COLLIDES** with meridian-transit timings (a classical transit-circle measurement). No static optical change inside A0 clears D3 or D6: each collision is with a measurement already made.

**D7. Rāhu's occultation, tested under the same window (guard 2b). COLLIDES on shape; timing survives only if a new axiom is added.**

  - The hand is 200 yojanas broad and the mouth 200 across, against a 50-yojana sun. The dark region at totality would be at least 4 times the sun's diameter. It would hide the chromosphere, the prominences and the inner corona (1–4 R☉).

The occulting edge is a circular arc whose Baily's beads match lunar-limb topography from LRO and Kaguya altimetry. **COLLIDES.**
  - A 50-yojana finger-joint could match the sun's size, but a finger has straight sides, not the measured circular limb. **COLLIDES.**
- (b) Width of the totality zone. An occulter 200 yojanas wide lying Δ yojanas below a 50-yojana sun casts an umbra at the ground of width w ≈ 200 + 150·H/Δ. With Δ ≤ 100, w ≥ 63,000 yojanas, larger than Jambudīpa (10,000, recalled). Measured totality paths are at most about 270 km wide. **COLLIDES** for any yojana longer than about 4.3 m.
- (c) Lunar eclipse. An occultation (mouth, cheek, hand) predicts the moon hidden, or a silhouette shaped like a hand. **COLLIDES** with what is measured:

  - To hold, physics would need an ADDED axiom (not textual): "Rāhu seizes only at nodal syzygies". With it, timing is indistinguishable by construction. Without it, **COLLIDES.**
  - [X] Jyotiṣa's later identification of Rāhu with the lunar node is a cross-tradition import, not a rescue.
- (e) SN 2.9–2.10, release after a verse. Generalised to "eclipse duration responds to agency", it COLLIDES: modern contact times match ephemeris plus limb profile to about a second. The two single past events themselves: **none — no measurement is available (historical)**. That makes them untestable, which is not the same as philosophy.

**D8. The circumference rule C = 3D (B2). COLLIDES under A0, and is mathematically impossible.**
- Plain English:
  - Read under A0 as physical lengths, C = 3D would need curved space. On a sphere of radius R, C/D = 3 exactly when r/R = π/6 (since sin(π/6)/(π/6) = 3/π). That gives R ≈ 47.7 yojanas for the sun and about 1.15 × 10⁶ for the cakkavāḷa.
  - No single constant curvature does both. With R large enough for the cakkavāḷa, the sun's circumference comes out about 157, not 150.
  - In any Riemannian space, small circles have C/D → π.
- Maths:
  - C(D) = 2πR sin(D/2R) ≤ 2πR
  - C(1,203,450) = 3,610,350 ⇒ R ≥ 574,600 ⇒ C(50) = 157.08 ± 10⁻⁶ ≠ 150
- **COLLIDES** with circle metrology: any wheel measured with a tape gives π to metrology precision.
- Reading it as a reckoning convention is OURS, not the text's (abh01t.tik.xml:2013 gives no approximation marker). Tier locates this; it does not rescue it.

## 3. Where the axioms contradict each other

2. B3 puts an opaque (B6) moon under the sun every new moon. A18's world darkens the sun only through Rāhu, on occasion. This is a tension, and it depends on B6, which is ours.
3. B3's gradual waxing (one day, "like a line") against D4's jump to about half in about 4 minutes, on the texts' own numbers.
4. B2 (C = 3D at 49, 50 and 1,203,450) is inconsistent with every constant-curvature geometry. This is a mathematical contradiction, not a textual one.
